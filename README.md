# CS-330-Computational-Graphics-and-Visualization

## How do I approach designing software?

Throughout this course, I learned that designing software involves more than deciding what the finished product should look like.
It requires breaking a larger idea into smaller and more manageable components. For my final project, I created a 3D coffee station 
based on a real-world reference. I had to determine how objects such as the countertop, wooden platform, canisters, cups, cream 
pitcher, backsplash, and cabinets could be recreated by combining basic 3D shapes. This helped me develop stronger skills in planning 
object placement, scale, transformations, textures, materials, lighting, and the overall composition of a scene.

My design process was incremental. I started with the major objects and basic geometry before concentrating on smaller details. Once 
the objects were in place, I continued improving their proportions, positions, textures, materials, and lighting. Comparing the rendered 
scene to my reference also helped me identify areas that needed improvement. This approach taught me the value of creating a working 
foundation first and refining it instead of trying to make everything perfect at once. I can apply this same strategy to future software 
projects by breaking complex requirements into smaller components and improving each component through testing and revision.

## How do I approach developing programs?

One of the most important development strategies I used during this project was iterative development. Rather than making a large number 
of changes at once, I learned to modify the scene in smaller steps, compile and run the program, examine the results, and then make 
additional adjustments. This was especially useful when working with transformations and lighting because even small changes to position, 
scale, rotation, or light values could significantly affect the appearance of the scene.

Iteration played a major role throughout the project. There were times when objects were floating above the countertop, extending through 
other objects, positioned too far back, or receiving light from an unexpected direction. Instead of treating these problems as failures, I 
used the rendered results to determine what needed to change in the code. As I progressed through the milestones, I became more comfortable 
troubleshooting individual parts of the scene instead of changing several unrelated values at the same time. My development approach has 
evolved into a more organized cycle of implementing, testing, observing, and refining. This is a strategy I can continue using in other 
programming projects because it makes debugging easier and reduces the possibility of introducing additional problems.

## How can computer science help me in reaching my goals?

Computational graphics and visualization have expanded my understanding of how programming can be used to create interactive visual 
environments. Before this course, much of my programming experience focused on code that processed information or supported traditional 
application interfaces. Working with OpenGL introduced me to concepts such as 3D coordinate systems, transformations, meshes, textures, 
materials, shaders, lighting, and camera movement. It also demonstrated how mathematics and programming work together to produce something 
that can be immediately seen and evaluated.

These skills will be useful as I continue my education because they have strengthened my understanding of problem-solving, debugging, modular 
development, and working with unfamiliar technologies. Professionally, even if I do not work specifically in computer graphics, the development
practices I gained from this course can transfer to software engineering. Building the final scene required me to take a visual goal, translate 
it into technical requirements, implement those requirements in code, and repeatedly improve the result. That process is similar to the way 
software developers turn user or client requirements into functional applications. Overall, this course has given me another area of computer 
science experience while strengthening the programming and problem-solving skills I can carry into future projects.
